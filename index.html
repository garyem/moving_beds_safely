Moving Bed Components Safely

Practical guidance for newly appointed Driver Technicians

Why This Matters

Some bed components can weigh approximately 19–24 kg and may need to be carried through confined spaces, around corners, and up staircases. Good planning and handling techniques can help reduce fatigue, improve safety and prevent damage to customer property.

Component Weights

Component Approx. Weight
Head End 22.8 kg
Foot End 22.8 kg
Head End Deck 23.5 kg
Foot End Deck 18.6–19.8 kg
Always consider not only the weight of the component but also the route it must travel.

Before You Lift

  • Is the route clear?
  • Are there any trip hazards?
  • Are doors already open?
  • Is there enough space to manoeuvre?
  • Is assistance required?
A few minutes of planning can prevent a difficult lift becoming an unsafe lift.

When To Seek Assistance

Consider requesting assistance when:

  • The load feels difficult to control.
  • The staircase is particularly narrow.
  • The staircase has multiple turns.
  • The route contains obstacles.
  • You are fatigued.
  • You are unsure whether the lift can be completed safely.
Seeking assistance is a sign of good judgement, not weakness.

Staircase Considerations

  • Check width restrictions.
  • Identify turning points.
  • Check lighting.
  • Remove obstacles where possible.
  • Consider where rest points may be needed.
Never rush a staircase lift.

Dog-Legged Staircases

Dog-legged staircases create additional challenges because the load may need to be rotated, visibility can be reduced, awkward postures may occur, and grip positions may need to change.

Take extra time to plan how the component will be turned at the landing before beginning the lift.

Using Rest Points

  • Identify safe locations to pause.
  • Maintain control of the component.
  • Communicate clearly with any assisting colleague.
A short pause can often improve control and reduce strain.

Team Lifts

  • Agree the route first.
  • Agree who leads the lift.
  • Communicate before changing direction.
  • Communicate before lowering the load.
  • Move at the pace of the slowest person.
Good communication is often more important than strength.

Common Mistakes

  • Twisting while carrying.
  • Rushing stair movements.
  • Carrying when fatigued.
  • Poor communication during team lifts.
  • Attempting awkward manoeuvres without planning.
  • Focusing only on weight rather than route complexity.

Customer Communication

Useful phrases:

  • “We’ll just take a moment to plan the safest route.”
  • “This section is heavier and needs careful manoeuvring.”
  • “We may need to reposition before moving upstairs.”
Clear communication helps manage expectations and creates a professional experience.

Escalation Routes

Stop and seek guidance if:

  • The route appears unsafe.
  • Access is more restricted than expected.
  • The component cannot be controlled safely.
  • There is a risk of injury or property damage.
If in doubt, stop and seek advice before proceeding.
